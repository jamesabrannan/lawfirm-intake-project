what is a USCIS receipt notice?

Assistant

A USCIS receipt notice is an acknowledgment that U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services received an application or petition. It commonly comes on Form I-797C, Notice of Action.

It generally identifies:

  • The application received.
  • The date USCIS received it.
  • A receipt number used to track the case.

A receipt is not an approval. Think of it as “we received your application,” rather than “we granted what you requested.” USCIS explanation.

In your demo, Mateo’s receipt acknowledges his Form I-765 application for employment authorization, commonly called a work permit.

Elena would only have a receipt if she had submitted an application or petition that generated one. Her immigration court proceedings alone do not establish that she should have a USCIS receipt—which is why requesting one in her current scenario lacks a clear reason.

Assistant

Yes. The usual term is an “internal legal research memorandum,” often shortened to “research memo” or “legal memo.” Preparing one is a standard legal research and writing task. It identifies legal questions, summarizes relevant facts, researches the governing law, and applies that law to the client’s circumstances—including unfavorable authorities and uncertainty. CUNY’s law-office memorandum guide.

What you describe also includes case progress and next steps, so a good title for your demo would be:

Draft Internal Legal Research Memorandum — Mateo: Pending Reckless-Driving Charge and Employment Authorization

“Research report” is understandable, but research memorandum is the more recognizable legal term. “Brief” can suggest an advocacy document submitted to a court, whereas your proposed memo would objectively inform the attorney’s decisions. Georgetown’s comparison.

For your demo, I would organize it like this:

Section What it would contain
Matter status and work completed Application receipt received; traffic documents reviewed; trial scheduled; medical evidence outstanding. Record only actions actually completed.
Questions presented Could the pending charge or a later disposition affect his employment authorization? Could the reported diabetic episode affect the traffic case?
Facts and sources Separate facts shown in documents, allegations in the citation, client statements, and information still unknown.
Preliminary answers Concise findings, with qualifications where facts or legal research are incomplete.
Applicable law and analysis Relevant immigration rules, Maryland reckless-driving law, and judicial decisions applied to the facts.
Missing evidence and further research Application category, medical records, driving evidence, court disposition, and authorities still requiring verification.
Recommended next steps Proposed actions for attorney review, with responsibility and deadlines where known.

That follows the conventional memo structure while adding the practical status information you want. Cornell’s description of a legal memorandum.

  1. Traffic matter: What must Maryland prove? Could evidence of sudden hypoglycemia affect proof of the alleged offense or support another argument? What do relevant decisions actually hold, and how closely do their facts match?
  2. Immigration matter: What employment-authorization category is involved? How are pending charges, convictions, and other dispositions treated under the rules applicable to that category? Does the medical evidence have any legally relevant role?

Assistant

A realistic manual process would start after the intake handoff, with an attorney defining the research assignment. A paralegal could gather records, research, and prepare a draft under attorney supervision; the attorney reviews the analysis and decides what to do.

For Mateo, the flow would be:

1. Review the intake and define the questions

The attorney reviews the intake notes and available documents, then specifies the research scope:

Determine whether Mateo’s pending Maryland reckless-driving charge could affect his employment-authorization application, and whether his reported diabetic episode could be relevant to the traffic allegation.

The assignment identifies the intended reader, deadline, and expected output: an internal research memorandum.

2. Establish the facts and current status

The researcher reads the source documents and creates a working chronology. Each entry distinguishes:

  • Documented information: application receipt date, charged offense, scheduled trial.
  • Allegations: the officer’s description of the driving.
  • Client statements: confusion, diabetes, and possible medical records.
  • Unknown information: employment-authorization category, underlying immigration matter, whether he was arrested, and whether traffic counsel is involved.

They also record what staff have actually done. “Client identified possible medical records” must not become “medical records requested” unless someone requested them.

3. Identify and obtain missing information

Staff clarify the application category and request relevant records through the firm’s approved procedures. That might include application copies, court records, and—with appropriate authorization—medical records.

Research can begin while records are outstanding, but the memo must identify conclusions that depend on those missing facts.

4. Research the two legal tracks

The researcher examines:

Track Research focus
Maryland traffic law Elements of the charged offense; relevant decisions concerning driving conduct, sudden incapacity, and foreseeability; evidence needed to evaluate those issues
Immigration and employment authorization Rules applicable to Mateo’s actual application category; treatment of pending charges and possible dispositions; disclosure and documentation requirements

They prioritize statutes, regulations, official agency guidance, and judicial decisions. They distinguish Maryland criminal cases from civil cases and decisions from other jurisdictions.

5. Verify authorities and keep a research log

For every potentially useful authority, record its citation, relevant passage, holding, jurisdiction, and relevance to Mateo. Check whether it remains valid and whether later decisions limit or reject it, typically using a legal citator such as Shepard’s or KeyCite.

Include unfavorable authorities. Distinguish effective law from proposed rules and record the research date.

6. Draft the internal research memorandum

A conventional structure would be:

  1. To / From / Date / Re / Research current through
  2. Questions presented
  3. Brief answers
  4. Relevant facts and procedural status
  5. Applicable law and analysis
  6. Limitations and outstanding information
  7. Proposed next steps

The analysis applies the law to the known facts and addresses counterarguments. It should explain uncertainty rather than manufacture a definite answer. This follows the objective approach described in CUNY’s law-office memorandum guide.

7. Check the draft against sources

Before handoff, the researcher checks every factual assertion against the file and every legal proposition against its authority. Dates, quotations, citations, and case outcomes receive particular attention.

For Mateo, that includes ensuring the memo never treats the pending charge as a conviction or the reported diabetic episode as medically established.

8. Attorney reviews and requests revisions

The attorney evaluates the legal analysis, identifies additional research or evidence needed, and considers coordination with traffic-defense counsel. The researcher revises the memo as directed.

A completed draft is draft_ready, not attorney-approved.

9. Approve, save, and assign follow-up

Once approved, staff save the final version in the matter file with its sources and research date. The attorney assigns any follow-up actions and deadlines. New medical evidence, a court disposition, or a material legal change may require an updated memo.

For your demo, the manual process establishes a useful boundary: the research memorandum informs the attorney’s decision; it does not itself decide Mateo’s case or authorize a filing.
